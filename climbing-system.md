## Philosophy & Rules

### Scope and Intent
This is our shared **Personal Operating Manual** for outdoor climbing.

We use it to:
- Maintain an **audit-grade list** of what we own.
- Standardize **how we rack, place, and use gear**.
- Teach each other using **consistent language and repeatable flows**.
- Reduce error by codifying what “normal” looks like.

#### Styles Covered
- Top-rope climbing (primary)
- Sport climbing (supported by gear; conservative use)
- Rappelling
- Anchor building and cleaning
- Self-belay / solo top-rope (documented with explicit constraints)
- Scrambling / approach safety

---

### Safety Doctrine (Non-Negotiables)

#### Redundancy Philosophy
- We avoid **single-point failure** wherever feasible.
- We prefer **two independent barriers** between us and catastrophe.
- We treat “it’s probably fine” as a hazard indicator.

#### Conservative Assumptions
- Anchors may be worn or misused.
- Hardware may be sun-aged.
- We may be tired, hot, or distracted.
- Dropped gear is possible.

#### Explicit Exclusions
- We do not lead trad.
- We do not multi-pitch.
- We do not simul-climb.
- We do not lead solo.
- We do not rappel without a backup.

#### Core Rules
- Do not climb outdoors without helmets.
- Do not rappel without an autoblock.
- Autoblock is always **below the device**, clipped to a leg loop.
- Do not cross-load carabiners at the edge.
- Do not knot Dyneema.
- Tie knots in rope ends near rope-length limits.
- Do not use damaged textiles.

## Transitions (High-Risk States)

### Purpose
Transitions are the **highest-risk moments** in climbing.

> If a system is changing, risk is elevated — regardless of difficulty.

### Core Transition Invariants
1. Never transition from **two attachments to zero**
2. Change **only one variable at a time**
3. Life-support devices do **not** change sides
4. If unsure, stop and revert to last known safe state
5. Unclipping primary systems requires verbal confirmation

### Mandatory Stop Points
Required whenever:
- A belay device is removed
- A rope is removed
- A tether is adjusted
- Heat, fatigue, or distraction is present

**Stop Point Protocol**
1. Pause
2. State aloud:
   - “Attached by ___”
   - “Primary device is ___”
   - “Backup is ___”
3. Proceed only after confirmation

### Common Transition Scenarios

#### Belay → Rappel
- Tether remains clipped until rappel is built, backed up, and weighted

#### Anchor Cleaning
- Two attachments before altering anchor
- Never untethered while re-threading

#### Temporary Tethering
- Locking carabiner only
- Never a belay substitute

#### Device Swap
- New device installed first
- Backup added
- Old device removed last

#### Passing Devices
- Passed clipped or locked
- Receiver confirms verbally

### Heat & Fatigue Modifiers (AZ)
- Slow transitions deliberately
- Increase verbalization
- Default to additional redundancy
- Abort earlier than usual

**Mantra:**  
**Stop → State → Check → Proceed**

## Safety Guidelines

### Common Climbing Hazards
- **Loose Rock**: Always test holds before trusting them, especially in sandstone areas like Camelback Mountain.
- **Weather Changes**: Check the forecast and be prepared for sudden rain or temperature drops.
- **Gear Failure**: Inspect all gear for wear and damage before each climb.
- **Human Error**: Double-check knots, harnesses, and belay systems with your partner.
- **Wildlife**: Be cautious of snakes, scorpions, and other wildlife common in Arizona.

### Pre-Climb Checklist
1. Inspect all gear (harness, rope, carabiners, etc.).
2. Check the weather forecast.
3. Review the climbing route and descent plan.
4. Perform a partner check:
   - Harness buckles secured.
   - Knots tied correctly.
   - Belay device set up properly.
   - Helmet on and adjusted.
5. Ensure you have enough water and snacks.

### Post-Climb Checklist
1. Inspect gear for damage or wear.
2. Coil ropes and organize gear.
3. Log any incidents or near-misses in the incident log.
4. Review the climb and discuss any lessons learned.
5. Ensure the area is clean and free of trash.

## Localized Climbing Information

### McDowell Mountains
- **Location**: Scottsdale, Arizona
- **Rock Type**: Granite
- **Climbing Style**: Sport and trad climbing with a variety of routes for different skill levels.
- **Popular Routes**: Tom’s Thumb, Sven Slab, and Gardener’s Wall.
- **Tips**:
  - Best season: Fall, winter, and early spring to avoid extreme heat.
  - Bring plenty of water and sun protection.
  - Watch for loose rock on less-traveled routes.

### Camelback Mountain
- **Location**: Phoenix, Arizona
- **Rock Type**: Sandstone
- **Climbing Style**: Bouldering and roped climbing with challenging routes.
- **Popular Routes**: The Praying Monk and Echo Canyon.
- **Tips**:
  - Early mornings are best to avoid crowds and heat.
  - Be cautious of fragile sandstone holds.
  - Parking can be limited, so plan accordingly.

### General Arizona Climbing Tips
- **Season**: Climbing is best during cooler months (October to April).
- **Wildlife**: Be aware of snakes, scorpions, and other desert wildlife.
- **Leave No Trace**: Pack out all trash and respect the natural environment.
- **Resources**: Check platforms like Mountain Project and TheCrag for detailed route information and updates.
